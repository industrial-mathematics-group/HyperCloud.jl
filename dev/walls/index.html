Walls and Moving Boundaries

HyperCloudCore treats a boundary through ghost particles: a MirrorBC evaluates the interior solution at a ghost's mirror point behind the wall and hands that state, with the wall's normal, position and velocity (WallPoint), to an operator that turns it into the ghost state. HyperCloud supplies the operators of the physical walls; the geometry (shapes, normals, moving pieces) lives in HyperCloudCore (Geometric Domains, Moving Boundaries).

ConditionEquationGhost state
slip_wall() (MirrorBC(SlipWall()))Eulerdensity of the mirrored state, velocity reflected relative to the wall: u' = u − 2 ((u − w)·n) n, pressure with the gradient of an accelerating wall: p' = p − 2 d ρ (n·a)
slip_wall()BGK, BGK mixture (species by species)specular reflection f'(v) = f(v − 2 ((v − w)·n) n): an exact node flip for a wall along an axis whose normal velocity is the grid centre; otherwise the equilibrium of the reflected state plus the interpolated non-equilibrium part, with zero mass flux through the wall
diffuse_wall(θ_w; velocity, accommodation) (MirrorBC(DiffuseWall(…)))BGK, BGK mixture (species by species: θ_w is the thermodynamic wall temperature, species s is emitted at k_B θ_w / m_s)velocities out of the wall: the discrete Maxwellian at θ_w and the wall velocity, with the density that returns the incident mass flux; velocities into the wall: the mirrored interior values. accommodation < 1 mixes in the specular reflection

Ghosts behind the corners and edges of a box are reflected in the face whose tag they carry (HyperCloudCore's ghost geometry), so a uniform flow along slip walls survives the corners with an inflow and an outflow exactly. All of them need a shape-based domain (:Grid_domain => :rectangular, :spherical or :shape); the walls of a box are tagged 2d − 1 / 2d per axis, so :Grid_bc => Dict(1 => FixedDirichlet(), 2 => slip_wall()) is a tube with an inflow on the left and a wall on the right. degree and bounded of the underlying MirrorBC are keyword arguments of both constructors.

Moving walls

A Moving piece of the shape carries its ghosts along and the cloud makes way for it (HyperCloudCore places the ghosts at every stage time and sweeps the interior after every step). The wall velocity, acceleration, spin and curvature reach the operators through the WallPoint: the slip wall reflects the normal velocity relative to the wall, and at an accelerating, rotating or curved wall gives the mirrored state the normal momentum of the fluid along the wall, ∂p/∂n = −ρ (n·a + 2 n·Ω(u − w) − u_t·H u_t), with the tangential velocity of an irrotational flow (see SlipWall; the even mirror, ∂p/∂n = 0, left a first-order error next to an oscillating piston and at static curved walls, and the material acceleration alone stirred a gas at rest around a spinning disc); the diffuse wall emits at the wall velocity. A piston driven into a gas at rest:

piston = Moving(Tagged(HalfSpace((1.0,), (1.0,)), 2), translating((-0.5,)))   # face at x = 1 − 0.5 t
tube   = intersect(Tagged(Cuboid((0.0,), (1.5,)), 1), piston)                  # the canvas reaches beyond the face
params[:Grid_domain] = :shape
params[:Grid_shape]  = tube
params[:Grid_mins], params[:Grid_maxs] = (0.0,), (1.5,)
params[:Grid_bc]     = Dict(1 => slip_wall(), 2 => slip_wall())

The canvas must reach beyond a moving face: a face on the end of the canvas shares its ghosts with the static wall there, and the grid refuses it. Keep the step below a quarter spacing per step for the piece (|w| Δt ≤ Δx / 4; the CFL number does not see the wall speed).

Caching. The motion of a Moving piece is a function, and the parameter hash reduces functions to "Closure": two runs that differ only in the motion of a shape instance share one cache file, and the second returns the first's result. Give the shape as a string of code instead, which the hash sees in full and in which T is the run's float type (so it works in Float32 too); euler_1d_piston does this:

params[:Grid_shape] = "intersect(Tagged(Cuboid((T(0),), (T(1.5),)), 1), Moving(Tagged(HalfSpace((T(1),), (T(1),)), 2), translating((-T(0.5),))))"

The runners check the wall operators before the first step: an operator without a method for the equation (SlipWall on linear advection, DiffuseWall on Euler) or a sliding velocity of the wrong length is an ArgumentError naming the boundary tag, not a MethodError from inside the kernel.

What was measured

  • Euler, shock reflection (gas at u = 1 against a slip wall, MUSCL2 VK, 200 particles, t = 0.3): the plateau behind the reflected shock is ρ = 2.0774, p = 2.9267, u = 4·10⁻⁴ against the Rankine–Hugoniot state 2.0792, 2.9266, 0 (p within 10⁻⁴, ρ within 2·10⁻³).
  • Euler, piston (w = 0.5 into a gas at rest, 300 particles over [0, 1.5], t = 0.3; the example euler_1d_piston): ρ = 1.4895, u = −0.5000, p = 1.7602 against 1.4899, −0.5, 1.7603, and the first particle stays within one spacing of the face.
  • BGK, equilibrium: a gas in equilibrium between diffuse walls at its temperature (also specular walls, or an accommodation of 0.5) stays in equilibrium to 2·10⁻¹⁶: the discrete flux balance of the diffuse wall is exact on the symmetric velocity grid. The same holds next to a curved wall (a gas at rest in a disc, 24² particles, 8² velocities, t = 0.1): 2·10⁻¹⁵ for both walls (the specular wall used to take the nearest velocity node off the axes, which drifted by 9 %).
  • BGK, specular reflection off the node grid (an equilibrium at u = (0.2, −0.1), 12² velocities, oblique normals): zero mass flux through the wall to round-off, density, velocity and energy of the reflected state within 2·10⁻⁴ (exact for the diagonal, which permutes the nodes); a moving wall reflects u' = 2w − u and a grid centred off the wall velocity (:PDE_vcenter) no longer lets mass through.
  • BGK, hot wall (θ_w = 2 on a gas at θ = 1, τ = 10⁻², t = 0.5): the temperature next to the wall reaches 1.83 / 1.86 / 1.87 for 50 / 100 / 200 particles (a temperature jump remains at this Knudsen number); the mass of the gas (trapezoid rule) changes by 1.15 / 0.71 / 0.42 % (0.24 % at 400 particles: order 0.7–0.8), against −3.9·10⁻⁵ for specular walls on a gas with a pressure jump (second order). The zero-flux condition holds for the ghost state, but the scheme's flux through the wall comes from a reconstruction across the half-range jump of the ghost, so a diffuse wall conserves mass at first order only; exact conservation would need a flux-form wall.
  • BGK, piston (τ = 10⁻⁴, isothermal diffuse face at the gas temperature, 300 particles): the plateau is within 1 % of the Euler Rankine–Hugoniot state (ρ = 1.482, p = 1.747, u = −0.493); the isothermal face adds a thin thermal layer that the Euler plateau does not have.

API

HyperCloud.SlipWall — Type
SlipWall()

Operator of a reflecting (slip, adiabatic) wall for MirrorBC: slip_wall builds the condition.

  • Euler: the normal velocity relative to the wall is reflected, u' ⋅ n = 2 w ⋅ n - u ⋅ n with the wall velocity w (zero for a static wall), so the normal velocity at the wall equals the wall's. At a flat, static or uniformly moving wall that is all (density, pressure and tangential velocity are mirrored evenly). At an accelerating, rotating or curved wall the even mirror (∂p/∂n = 0) is first order; the ghost's pressure then takes the normal momentum of the fluid along the wall, p' = p + 2 d ∂p/∂n with the ghost's wall distance d and ∂p/∂n = -ρ (n ⋅ a + 2 n ⋅ Ω (u - w) - u_t ⋅ H u_t) (the WallPoint's acceleration a, spin Ω and curvature H, u_t the tangential velocity relative to the wall), extrapolated log-linearly (always positive; the exponent 2 d ∂p/∂n / p bounded to ±1/2), its density follows isentropically and its tangential velocity that of a flow along the curved wall that is irrotational in the lab frame, u_t' = u_t - 2 d (H u_t - P_t(Ωᵀ n)) relative to the wall (Dadone & Grossman's curvature-corrected symmetry, Galilean invariant). Measured: an oscillating piston 0.18 % of the wave amplitude at 2400 particles (first order with the even mirror); a disc spinning in a gas at rest keeps it at rest to 1e-15 (the material acceleration alone stirred it by 1 %); the potential vortex between static circles, L1 error of ρ 1.6e-3 / 6.8e-4 / 2.1e-4 for N = 50 / 100 / 200 (order 1.3–1.7; 1.2e-2 / 6.2e-3 / 3.5e-3 with the even mirror) and the near-wall velocity error eight times smaller. The tangential correction is bounded by half the relative tangential speed, like the pressure exponent.
  • BGK: specular reflection of the distribution, f'(v) = f(v - 2 ((v - w) ⋅ n) n). Exact on the tensor velocity grid when the reflection maps the grid onto itself: a wall normal along an axis whose normal wall velocity equals the grid centre on that axis (:PDE_vcenter; a static wall on a grid centred at 0), where the node index is flipped. Otherwise (oblique or curved walls, moving walls, a shifted grid) the reflected velocity is not a node: the reflection is split into the discrete equilibrium of the reflected macroscopic state (density and internal energy kept, velocity reflected as for Euler), which is exact, and the non-equilibrium rest of the distribution, interpolated (multilinearly on the tensor grid) at the reflected velocities. The components leaving the wall are then scaled so that the net mass flux through the wall vanishes, as for DiffuseWall. A gas at rest next to a curved wall is reflected exactly, a drifting equilibrium at an oblique wall up to the zero-flux scaling (2e-4 to 2e-3 in the moments, shrinking with the velocity grid); the interpolated non-equilibrium part is clipped at zero (it made the ghost negative for strongly non-equilibrium gases, -10 % of max f for two beams at an oblique wall), so the reflection stays non-negative.

HyperCloud.DiffuseWall — Type
DiffuseWall(temperature; velocity = nothing, accommodation = 1)

Operator of a diffusely reflecting wall at the temperature θ_w = p / ρ for the BGK model, for MirrorBC (diffuse_wall builds the condition). The components of the ghost state whose velocity points out of the wall into the fluid (relative to the wall, (v - w) ⋅ n < 0) are the discrete Maxwellian at the wall temperature and velocity, with the density that makes the mass flux through the wall vanish: the incident flux of the interior distribution at the wall point returns (the fit of the mirror image evaluated at the wall, uses_wall_state); the components pointing into the wall keep the mirrored interior values. Balanced at the ghost's mirror point instead, the returning density differed from ghost to ghost by the depth of its mirror point, which doubled the scheme's mass flux through the wall out of equilibrium (the 1-D tube between a cold gas and warm walls at N = 50/100/200/400: 1.15e-2/7.1e-3/4.2e-3/2.4e-3 of the mass before, 7.0e-3/3.6e-3/1.9e-3/9.8e-4 now). What remains is the first-order mismatch between the ghost's balance point and the depth of its interior partners in the per-velocity upwind flux; the specular wall, an exact node flip, conserves to the scheme's order. The wall velocity w is the piece's velocity from the WallPoint plus velocity (a tangential sliding velocity, e.g. the plates of a Couette flow; a tuple of length D). With an accommodation coefficient α < 1 (Maxwell's model) the emitted distribution is α times the diffuse one plus 1 - α times the specular reflection of SlipWall.

Isothermal: a wall at the gas temperature reflects an equilibrium gas at rest into itself exactly (the discrete flux balance is exact on the symmetric velocity grid), a colder or hotter wall drives a thermal boundary layer. Internal degrees of freedom (h) are emitted at the wall temperature as well.

HyperCloud.slip_wall — Function
slip_wall(; degree = 1, bounded = true) -> MirrorBC

A reflecting wall (Euler: slip, adiabatic; BGK: specular): MirrorBC(SlipWall(); degree, bounded), see SlipWall and MirrorBC. Needs a shape-based domain (wall normals); follows a Moving piece.

HyperCloud.euler_1d_piston — Function
euler_1d_piston(; speed = 0.5, face = 1.0) -> SimulationConfig

A piston face starting at face and driven at speed into a gas at rest in the tube [0, 1.5] (the canvas reaches beyond the face), with the exact solution exact_piston as reference. The shape is given as a string (:Grid_shape), so the piston speed is part of the parameter hash and the run follows :T; the keys :piston_speed and :piston_face feed the reference. Change the speed through the keyword (shape and reference together), not by overriding :piston_speed alone.

source
HyperCloud.exact_piston — Function
exact_piston(params) -> (spacetime -> State)

Exact solution of euler_1d_piston: the gas at rest ahead of the shock, the Rankine–Hugoniot state moving with the piston behind it (and that state, for reference, beyond the piston face). Reads the gas state from the IC keys, γ, the piston speed :piston_speed and face position :piston_face from params.
