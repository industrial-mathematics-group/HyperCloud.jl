## Findings

### F1 (high, CONFIRMED) — `SlipWall`'s curvature correction of the tangential velocity uses the lab-frame velocity: first order at every translating curved wall; B16 twice as far from Galilean invariance as before, a translating spinning body 25× worse
`HyperCloud.jl src/BoundaryConditions/_main.jl:68-71`. The ghost's tangential velocity is `s * vt` with `vt` the
**lab-frame** tangential velocity and `s = √(1 − 4 d vt·H vt / |vt|²)`; the comment justifies the lab frame with the
spinning disc (gas at rest is irrotational in the lab frame, not relative to the disc). The correct normal derivative of
the tangential velocity of an irrotational lab-frame flow at a rigidly moving wall is (2-D; derived from the symmetry of
∇v, `(n·∇)t = 0` for the SDF frame, and `v·n = w·n` along the wall)

  `∂v_t/∂n = −κ (v_t − w_t) + n·Ω t`,  κ = t·H t,

i.e. the relative tangential velocity plus a spin term. The lab-frame form `−κ v_t` coincides with it only when
`κ w_t = −n·Ω t`, i.e. for a pure rotation about the centre of curvature (the disc of B7c). For a translating wall it is
off by `κ w_t`: the condition is not Galilean invariant and the ghost carries an O(d κ |w_t|) error (first order).
- Operator level (`probe_slip_op.out`, `probe_slip_fix.out`): the homentropic vortex next to a circle, static: error/d²
  = 2.0 (order 2); the same flow shifted by V = (0.3, 0.2) with the circle translating at V (an exact solution): error
  3.5e-4 at d = 0.005, error/d² growing 1.2 → 14 (order 1); inner disc: error/d² 18 → 48. With the corrected formula
  (`u_t' = w_t + s_rel u_rt + 2d (n·Ω t) t`, `s_rel` from the relative tangential velocity) every case is order 2 with
  the static-wall constants (also translation + spin, case (h)).
- Simulation X4 (`simroot/experiments/Boundary/x_disc_comoving_spin_2d.jl`, `eval_uniform.out`): a disc translating
  with the gas and spinning about its own centre must leave the uniform state exact. Round-off in the pressure terms
  (they cancel as for B7c) takes the `p2 != p` branch, which then rescales the lab-frame `vt = V_t ≠ 0`:
  max |u − U| = 0.12 / 0.074 (N = 100 / 200, VK), 0.078 / 0.056 (EPD1), against 4.6e-3 / 2.6e-3 with the parent
  (whose error was the centripetal term of finding 1). The same disc without spin (X1) stays exact because the early
  return `p2 == p` skips the correction — the switch is discontinuous in the data.
- B16 (3-D ball, static in a stream vs translating through a gas at rest, the report's criterion): the Galilean
  difference **doubles**: 1.10e-1 / 5.1e-2 at 40³ / 60³ against 6.1e-2 / 2.7e-2 with the parent (`check_new_b16.out`,
  `check_old_bnd2.out`). The static ball gets the curvature correction on the full stream velocity, the translating one
  on the lab-frame perturbation only, so the two runs now differ at first order. (Still under the 10 % threshold.)
- B7b (disc translating through a gas at rest): the Galilean comparison still passes (it averages over bins), but the
  minimum surface pressure of the moving disc now differs more from the static one (0.627 vs 0.648 at 400²; parent
  0.638 vs 0.651): the static disc gets the curvature correction on the full relative velocity, the moving one on the
  lab-frame perturbation only.
- The HyperCloud docstring and the Core docs state the lab-frame form as the design.
- Fix: correct the tangential velocity relative to the wall and add the spin term, in vector form
  `u_t' = w_t + (u_t − w_t) − 2d [H (u_t − w_t) − P_t(Ωᵀ n)]` (or the `√` form on the relative velocity, plus
  `2d (n·Ω t) t` in 2-D); that is Galilean invariant, exact for X1/X4 and B7c, and order 2 in the probes. Regression
  tests: the operator on the Galilean-shifted vortex (error/d² bounded) and X4 at 50² for a few steps.

### F2 (high, CONFIRMED) — supersonic flow past a static disc: the curvature term drives the lee-side ghosts to the pressure clamp and the run loses positivity
`_main.jl:61-71`. At a convex wall `∂p/∂n = ρ κ u_t²` with κ = −1/r, so `p2/p = 1 − (2d/r) γ M_t²`: with M_t ≈ 3–4 in
the expansion behind a cylinder and 2d/r ≈ 0.07 (h = 0.01, r = 0.15) the linear extrapolation reaches zero and the clamp
`p2 = 0.01 p` fires; the ghost then gets ρ2 = 0.037 ρ (isentropic from the clamp) and a tangential speed raised by
`s = √(1 + 4d/r)`. X5 (`simroot/experiments/Boundary/x_disc_mach2_2d.jl`: impulsive Mach 2 flow past the B7 disc, 200²,
t = 0.4, `runs3.out`): **RK2MUSCL2(VK) ends with negative pressure, min p = −2.1e-3 at (0.121, −0.091)** (on the wall,
lee side; the health check fails), RK3MUSCL3(EPD1) min p 2.6e-3; the parent (even mirror) has min p 2.7e-2 / 3.8e-2 at
the same place and passes. A static curved wall in a supersonic flow (cylinder, sphere, blunt body, shock diffraction
over a curved ramp) is a standard use; this is a robustness regression.
- The linearised terms are only valid while `2d |∂p/∂n| ≪ p` and `4d |κ| ≪ 1`; the 1 % clamp is far outside that range.
- Fix (suggestion): extrapolate log-linearly, `p2 = p exp(2d ∂p/∂n / p)` (same to second order, always positive, and
  the isentropic density follows smoothly), and/or limit the relative correction (e.g. `|p2/p − 1| ≤ 0.5`, falling back
  towards the even mirror beyond that) together with `s`; then rerun X5 and B13 (the limiter must not touch the smooth
  vortex). A regression test: X5 at 100² to t = 0.2 with positivity.

### F3 (medium, CONFIRMED) — finite-difference Hessian across the kinks of user shapes: garbage ghosts at corners
`HyperCloudCore src/Grid/Shapes.jl:696-705` (fallback) with `_ghost_geometry_curv` (`Domains.jl:641`). For a shape
without an analytic `_sdf_hessian` (every user shape: polygons, level sets from CAD, which the shapes docs advertise),
the second differences with step `eps^(1/4)` (1.2e-4) straddle the medial axis next to corners and return values of
order 1/h_fd. `probe_fd_hessian.out`: a square obstacle with an exact user SDF in a box, 264 ghosts of its tag, 12 of them
(behind/near the corners) store t·Ht between −1 and **−1.2e4**. With `slip_wall()` on a uniform flow
`u = (0.5, 0.2)` (`probe_square_slip.out`) those ghosts get p = 0.01 (the clamp), ρ = 0.037 (isentropic from the clamp)
and |u| up to 20.6 (`s = √(1 + 4 d·1.2e4)`), while the same obstacle as a built-in `Cuboid` gives the exact uniform
ghosts. On the parent the even mirror was exact for this case: a regression for user shapes with corners.
- Fix (any of): limit the stored curvature to what the lattice resolves (e.g. clamp |t·Ht| ≤ 1/(2d) or ≤ c/h; beyond
  that the correction is not meaningful), detect kinks (the one-sided second differences disagree, or
  `|∇φ(x ± h e)|` direction jumps) and store zero there, and/or evaluate the FD only for ghosts whose wall point is a
  smooth boundary point. A test: the user-square obstacle with a uniform flow must keep the uniform ghosts.

### F4 (low, CONFIRMED) — in 3-D the tangential correction only rescales |u_t|: first order for flows oblique to the principal curvature directions
`_main.jl:70-71`: `s * vt` scales the tangential vector by one factor from the normal curvature along `vt`. With an
anisotropic Hessian the correction must change the direction too (`−2d H u_t`). `probe_slip_3d.out`: inside a cylinder
(H = diag(1, 0) in the tangent plane) with a vortex Γ = 0.5 plus an axial flow W = 0.5: error 3.5e-3 at d = 0.005,
error/d = 0.7 (order 1; even mirror 1.0); |Δu_z| = 2.5e-3 because the axial velocity, which must not change, is scaled
with the swirl. Pure swirl or pure axial flow are fine. Among the built-in shapes only balls are curved (isotropic), so
this affects user shapes (FD Hessian) and seams; low. Fix: the vector form of F1 (`−2d H (u_t − w_t)`), which also
removes the `√`/clamp asymmetry.

### F5 (low, CONFIRMED) — FD step scaled with |x|: Float32 curvature of a user shape away from the origin is wrong
`Shapes.jl:697`: `h = eps(T)^(1/4) max(1, |x|∞)`. In Float32 that is 0.019 |x|; for a circle of radius 0.15 centred at
(10, 10) the step (0.19) exceeds the radius and t·Ht is off by 29 % (`probe_fd_hessian.out`; 3.8e-3 at the origin;
Float64 1.7e-5 at (10, 10)). Offset or dimensional geometries in Float32 (GPU runs) get wrong curvature terms. Fix: cap
the step by a fraction of the grid spacing (pass `h_snap` into `_ghost_geometry_curv`), or evaluate the differences in
Float64 for Float32 shapes (the Hessian is only computed on the host, at build and insertion time) — a Float32 SDF
evaluated at Float64 offsets still rounds, so the cap is the more robust part.

### F6 (low, CONFIRMED) — the regression test of d916089 cannot fail on the CPU

Also worth a line: the semantics on singular input changed (a zero pivot now fails with `info > 0`, StaticArrays returned

### d916089 — the CUDA Float64 diagnosis (checked, correct)
- Mechanism verified from the sources and by compiling to PTX locally: `Base.FastMath.sqrt_fast` → `sqrt_llvm_fast` →
  `call fast double @llvm.sqrt.f64`; CUDACore 6.5.1 has no device override for `sqrt_fast`; GPUCompiler 2.22.2 (the
  version in `env_bnd_cuda`) rewrites every `afn` `llvm.sqrt.f64` to `rcp.approx.ftz.d(rsqrt.approx.d(x))`
  (`PTXFSqrtFastPass`, "there is no native fast f64 sqrt") — `rcp.approx.ftz.f64` is a single-precision-class
  reciprocal, which matches the observed 1e-7…1e-6 relative fit errors and their absence in Float32 (where the
  `approx.f32` instructions are within a few ulp of Float32 anyway). `_cholesky_sym` compiles to `sqrt.rn.f64`.
- Other fast-math on the device paths: none found. No `@fastmath`/`*_fast` in HyperCloudCore or HyperCloud `src`;
  in StaticArrays only `cholesky.jl:42` uses `sqrt_fast` (`norm`, `normalize`, `\`, triangular solves, small `inv`,
  `qr`/`eigen` use plain `sqrt`/division); KernelAbstractions has none; `CUDABackend(fastmath = false)` is the default,
  so no job-wide `afn`. All eight `_cholesky_moment` call sites and the two MUSCL `cholesky` calls are converted; no
  other `cholesky`/`lu`/`qr` on a device path.
- Evidence: the probe logs show the constant fits at 2e-6 (degree 0) / 4–9e-5 (degree 1) on V100 and A30 before, and
  ≤ 2.3e-14 (= the CPU level) after; the 1-D acoustic case is 1.000000000 on CUDA after (25121026). The 2-D/3-D free
  streams 25121027/28 only print a 4-digit summary (min(u1) = 1.000, mass drift 0) in the log, so "identical to the CPU"
  is not visible there (not re-verifiable from the logs; the 1-D evidence suffices). The logs do not print the code
  commit of the vendored packages; the before/after pairs are consistent with the fix, though.
- B > 24 is now unrolled too (3-D degree 4 with intercept: B = 35): compiles in ~1 s, 0 allocations, accurate — and,
  unlike StaticArrays' heap fallback, usable in a kernel. An improvement.

  different piece types (no dynamic tuple indexing in the kernel). Tags > 63 throw on the host as before; the kernel's
  `1 <= k <= 64` guard is dead but harmless.
- Size (`probe_param_size.jl`): old 4992 B for a tag-7 ball (two `NTuple{8, GhostMotion{3}}`, matches the 5.03 KiB in
  job 25122731), growing 624 B per tag; new 64 B + 192 B per piece (256 B for one piece). Limit: **21 moving pieces in
  3-D reach 4096 B** (and > 127 pieces overflow the `Int8` table with an `InexactError`): low, worth a check with a
  clear error (or passing the placements in a small device array) if many-body problems are intended.
- Other kernels: no other launch passes per-tag or per-piece tuples; the ghost fit passes one `GhostMotion` (312 B in
  3-D) plus arrays, the BGK equation is small (1-D node/weight vectors), the MOOD/flux kernels take arrays and scalars.
  Nothing else near 4 KiB found by reading the launch sites (not measured on a device).

## Minor issues

- `_ghost_geometry_curv` evaluates H at `x − φ n` also for corner ghosts that store their own face's plane distance:
  that point is on the face's extension, not necessarily on the boundary (zero for boxes; for a ball meeting a face the
  ball's H is projected onto the face's tangent). Harmless for the built-in shapes, untested.
- `_corner_ghost` treats the whole surface of a moving piece as wall; a piece whose surface is partly inside the region
  (a moving piece intersected with or united to static shapes) could turn interior points next to the hidden part into
  ghosts when they are also within h/2 of a static wall (SUSPECTED, not constructed).
- `_snap_near_wall!` rebuilds `map(at_time, geom.pieces)` for every near-wall point (host, cheap).
- Concave walls with radius < 4d zero the ghost's tangential velocity (`max(0, …)` in `s`); fine for resolved walls.
- Commit 025b0df says "re-encoded when a piece rotates in 3-D" and the docs "passed through complements, transformations
  and moving pieces": accurate. 15277eb's numbers reproduce (B13 2.0e-3 / 6.9e-4 / 2.0e-4, near-wall 2.2e-2 / 1.6e-2 /
  8.2e-3, spinning disc 1e-15, piston 0.18 %).
- The review env's `Project.toml` `[sources]` point to older worktrees (walls-review, review-mb) while its Manifest points
  to the review worktrees; loading uses the Manifest (the stack traces of the probes point into `rr-retest` /
  `rr-walls-retest`), but a `Pkg.resolve` would switch.

## Verdict

The CUDA fix is right and well diagnosed (verified down to the PTX), complete for the device paths, and an improvement
for large bases. The corner-particle fix and the `place_ghosts!` slimming (888a0c4) are correct and behaviour-preserving.
The new normal-momentum pressure term is correctly derived and fixes the rotating-disc regression (B7c 1e-15) and most of
the static-curvature error (B13 L1(ρ) 17× smaller at 200², order 1.5–1.8). But the slip wall's new curvature treatment
is not ready: the tangential correction is done in the lab frame, which is right only for rotation about the centre of
curvature, so translating curved walls are first order, B16 is twice as far from Galilean invariance as before and a
translating spinning body 25× worse (F1); the linearised pressure/velocity correction has no safe range and makes a Mach 2
flow past a static disc lose positivity (F2); the FD curvature of user shapes explodes at corners (F3). F1–F3 should be
fixed before a release; F4–F7 are small. Core 29238/29238 (025b0df and 888a0c4), HyperCloud walls 76/76.
Findings: 2 high (F1, F2), 1 medium (F3), 4 low (F4, F5, F6 tests, F7 docs); all confirmed.
